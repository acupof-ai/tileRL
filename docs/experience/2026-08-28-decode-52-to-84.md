# How decode went 52.6 → 87.2 tok/s in one day — Qwen3.8-27B-NVFP4, one H20

Method record. Per-change entries live under `wins/` and `errors/`; this is
the loop that produced them, in the order it happened, with what each step
was worth and why it worked. Arle (agent-infer) is 84.5 tok/s on the same
card; we started at 52.6 and finished at 87.2.

## The loop

1. **A gate that cannot lie.** `tilerl bench --suite decode-kv,prefill,kv-reuse`
   (`scripts/bench_harness.py`): steady-state decode at 4 KV depths × B=1,8,
   prefill curve, prefix-cache hit-rate, tiny train; every row gated at
   ≥0.97× a committed snapshot (`wins/bench-baseline.json`), winners
   auto-raise it. Run-to-run noise 0.4% on a quiet host. Nothing shipped
   without a green run plus `verify_h20_fp4.py` checks 1–3 (greedy text).
2. **Price kernels where they run.** `scripts/profile_graph_kernels.py`:
   per-kernel GPU time inside the captured decode graph. `scripts/profile_glue.py`:
   every torch call in one eager tick attributed to its `file:line`. `ncu`
   on the real model for the kernel that dominates: DRAM vs SM-issue vs
   stalls, instructions per element.
3. **One change, one A/B, one number, then the gate.** Kill or ship the
   same hour; write the entry either way.

## What did not move it (measured, recorded under errors/)

- Scale dtype f32→bf16→e4m3: identical µs. Split-K ×2…×16: identical. Both
  assumed bandwidth-bound; ncu said issue-bound. (`errors/…-fp4-gemv-issue-bound-ncu.md`)
- "Kernel-count-bound": fusing 176 launches bought 2%. Eager cuda-event
  timings priced launch+sync that the graph already erases. (`errors/…-kernel-count-verdict-refuted.md`)
- In-place GDN state, first cut: 6.5 → 57 µs — read-after-write on one
  global buffer per element serializes the loop; stage in registers. (`errors/…-gdn-inplace-raw-serialized.md`)
- n_partition 2/8/16, int32 graph buffers, bf16 GDN a/b: neutral.

## The four rules this day licenses

1. **Below ~50% roofline is not automatically bandwidth-bound.** Run ncu
   first; if issue-busy ≳ 80% and DRAM ≲ 50%, cut instructions per element
   (packed decode, packed math), not bytes or blocks.
2. **Measure in-graph or under ncu.** The eager A/B harness floors at
   ~40 µs of CPU launch time and hid a real −22%; eager per-kernel means
   overstate small kernels ~10×.
3. **Glue is a first-class lever.** Attribute every torch call to a line;
   a parameter cast inside an op is a per-tick kernel. Store the served
   dtype, cache the cast, or fold it into the neighbour's epilogue.
4. **One timing job per host; loadavg stamped.** Another tenant's nvcc
   moved B=8 rows 60% with no GPU sharing.

## Where the last 0.7% and the next 2× are

Remaining B=1 tick 11.9 ms ≈ 78% GEMV (fp4 at 64% DRAM, fp8 at 61%, both
register-limited to 7 blocks/SM) + conv-window gather/scatter (~0.4 ms,
needs a double-buffered pool) + attention. B=8 still runs the padded WGMMA
path at 3–4× the M=1 GEMV's per-byte cost — the aggregate-throughput lever.
