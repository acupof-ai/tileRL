# The GDN prefill kernel leaves 38% of the SMs idle — and a doc says the opposite

## Context

Chasing prefill (2058 tok/s against sglang's 4022 at the same B=1; 4908 is its B=8), I A/B'd the prefill chunk
width, since a 2048 prompt at the 512 default is four forwards and every GEMM
therefore sees M=512.

| chunk | GPU-busy | kernels | tok/s | GEMMs | GDN |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 512 | 996.4 ms | 11,834 | 2055 | 290.4 + 250.6 ms | 320.6 ms (192 launches) |
| 1024 | 982.6 ms | 5,930 | 2084 | 275.4 + 236.8 ms | 347.0 ms (96) |
| 2048 | 971.6 ms | 2,978 | **2108** | 267.8 + 226.3 ms | 358.8 ms (48) |

+2.6% overall — under the 3% gate, so no default flip. But the two halves move
in OPPOSITE directions: the GEMMs gain 8-10% from the larger M, and GDN LOSES
12% as its launch count falls.

## Root Cause

`make_gdn_chunk_fused` launches `T.Kernel(NVH, B, threads=128)` — one block per
(value head, batch). At B=1 that is **48 blocks on an H20's 78 SMs: 0.62 waves,
38% of the machine idle in every GDN launch**, at any chunk width.

`docs/experience/errors/2026-08-25-gdn-chunked-gdr-rejected.md` states the
opposite — "the 48 value heads already saturate the SMs (78 on H20), so the
chunked pipeline's 8x more blocks don't add useful parallelism" — and that
sentence is part of why more-parallel formulations were rejected. 48 is not 78.
The chunked pipeline still lost for its own reasons (bf16 intermediates, six
stages), but the occupancy half of that verdict does not hold.

## What This Costs, And What It Would Take

Perfect SM fill would be ~78/48 = 1.63x on 32.1% of prefill, i.e. ~1.14x
overall. The recurrence permits it: state column `S[:,v]` depends only on `k`,
`beta` and `v_raw[v]`, so the V dimension is embarrassingly parallel and a
2-way V split needs no cross-block communication.

The epilogue does not permit it as cheaply: the gated RMSNorm reduces across
the whole V=128 vector of a head, so a V-split forces the norm and z-gate into
a second, small kernel. One launch becomes two.

Not attempted here — three consecutive GDN kernel rewrites have now failed to
beat the serial kernel, and this one cannot be compiled on a machine with no
GPU. Recorded so the next attempt starts from the occupancy number rather than
from a sentence that says the opposite.

### A second wrong occupancy argument — mine

I then talked myself out of the V split with wave arithmetic: 48 blocks is one
wave, 96 half-blocks is 1.23 waves, and two waves of half work equals one wave
of full work, so no gain. **That assumes one resident block per SM, and the
registers say otherwise.** The kernel holds a K=128 float state column per
thread: 128 threads x 128 registers = 16K of an SM's 64K, and a 64-thread half
block needs 8K, so an SM can host several. 96 blocks do not serialize into two
waves; they spread across all 78 SMs.

So the objection that killed it was the same shape as the objection it was
meant to replace — an occupancy claim asserted rather than computed.

### Measured, without writing a kernel

The kernel's grid is `(value heads, batch)`, so calling it directly with a
larger batch varies the blocks INSIDE one launch — which the engine never does,
because it admits one prefill row per tick. `scripts/occ_gdn.py`:

The first run of this probe called the kernel in f32. It is bf16-IO, so that
was 2.5x slower than what the engine issues and the numbers were not
comparable — the corrected probe reproduces the engine exactly (1.672 ms at
B=1/T=512 against the profile's 1670 us).

| blocks | ms/launch | work | effective speedup |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 48 | 1.672 | 1x | — |
| 96 | 2.015 | 2x | **1.67x** |
| 144 | 2.079 | 3x | **2.42x** |
| 192 | 3.970 | 4x | 1.69x |

Twice the work costs 1.20x the time; three times costs 1.24x. The SMs are not
merely under-filled, they are mostly idle.

A V split halves (or quarters) each block's work and multiplies the count, so
a 2-way split lands at 2.015/2 = 1.008 ms against 1.672 — **1.67x on the
kernel**. Against GDN's 33.3% of prefill that is **+13.4% overall**
(2110 -> ~2436 tok/s).

The f32 run had put this at 1.20x and 5.5%, which is why it was written off as
marginal. It is not marginal.

And the cost is per-step, not per-launch: us/step is flat at 3.08-3.28 across
T = 64, 128, 256, 512. A serial scan whose step cost does not amortise is
latency-bound, which is exactly the regime where more resident blocks help and
where changing the arithmetic (three chunkwise attempts) does not.

## Rule

An occupancy claim is two integers; check them. "48 heads saturate 78 SMs" sat
in a root-cause section for four days and steered at least one rejection, and
within the hour I replaced it with my own unchecked claim about waves. When a
doc — or you — explains a result with a resource being full, compute how full:
blocks against SMs, and registers against the register file, because the second
decides whether the first is even the limit.
