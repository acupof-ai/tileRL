# sglang's answer to our biggest kernel: fla, 6.8 ms where we spend 63 — 2026-08-29

> Status: measured, not yet adopted. `fla` is installed on the pod and its
> interface matches ours.

## Context

GDN is 27.6% of our prefill: `gdn_chunk_fused` runs a T-long **scalar scan**
inside one kernel and reads **0.13% of the tensor pipe** (ncu). Everything said
about closing that was my own estimate, and I revised it four times in one
session — 6.4x, then 2.7x, then a 2x-wide interval — each revision from
counting one side and pricing the other at zero.

The user's instruction was to go look at what sglang does. sglang runs
`flash-linear-attention` (fla), which is installed in the container.

## The number

`scripts/probe_fla_gdn.py`, our shapes exactly (b=1, T=512, 48 value heads,
DK=DV=128), H20 GPU 7:

| fla kernel | us / layer |
|---|---:|
| `chunk_gated_delta_rule_fwd_kkt_solve` | 62.4 |
| `chunk_gated_delta_rule_fwd_kernel_h_blockdim64` | 31.5 |
| `chunk_fwd_kernel_o` | 26.5 |
| `recompute_w_u_fwd` | 15.3 |
| `chunk_local_cumsum_scalar` | 2.9 |
| **one layer** | **140.7** |

**48 GDN layers: 6.8 ms against our 63 — 9.3x.** Faster even than the 9.9 ms
"pure matmul roofline" I computed, because that roofline timed cuBLAS `bmm`
per shape and paid a launch and a round-trip to memory for each; the fused
kernels do not.

Prefill: 229 - 63 + 6.8 = 173 ms, **2237 -> ~2960 tok/s (1.32x)**. Measured
from a real implementation rather than estimated from mine.

## What it does that we do not

Four kernels, not the five or six I had guessed:

1. `gdn_gate_chunk_cumsum` — `A_log`, `dt_bias`, softplus and the chunk-local
   cumsum in ONE kernel. I measured that glue at 18.0 us in torch; fused it is
   **2.9**.
2. `..._fwd_intra` — its own comment: *"fused kkt + solve_tril + recompute_w_u"*.
   The chunk interior never lands in memory.
3. `..._fwd_h` — the inter-chunk state scan.
4. `chunk_o` — the readout.

And the piece I had called "the crux, with no in-kernel primitive" —
`fla/ops/utils/solve_tril.py` — is a **16x16 blocked inversion**: the four
diagonal blocks are inverted by a 16-step row loop, and every off-diagonal
block comes from `tl.dot` (`Ai_21 = -Ai_22 A_21 Ai_11`, and so on). Serial
depth 16, not the 64 I feared, and the rest on the tensor cores.

It is still the largest single kernel (62.4 of 140.7 us, 44%), so "settle the
solve first" was right — but the answer to it already exists and is readable.

## Two things this corrects

- **My "the glue is 2.35x the matmuls, so only 2.7x" was wrong.** Fused, the
  gates cost 2.9 us where torch took 18.0. Glue measured in torch is not glue in
  a kernel; I had used one as evidence about the other, which is the same error
  that produced the speculation 1.06x prediction against a measured 0.57x.
- **`chunk_size=64` is fla's default** where our chunked backward had to drop to
  16 for f32 accuracy. Their `FLA_TRIL_PRECISION` env (`ieee`/`tf32`/`tf32x3`)
  shows the triangular inverse is exactly where that precision is decided, and
  they manage it explicitly instead of shrinking the chunk to avoid it.

## End to end it LOSES — and that is the actual finding

Wired behind `TILERL_GDN_FLA=1` at the one call site that is pure recurrence
(`gdn_chunk_core`, with our conv/silu/norms/gating unchanged around it), it
matches the serial core to **7.3e-03 on the output and 6.6e-03 on the state** —
bf16's own resolution, inside the repo's `rtol=1e-2` gate. Same maths.

Then, both arms back to back on a quiet host (control arm spread 0.0-0.2%, all
three of its rows PASSing at 0.998-1.002x of baseline):

| prefill len | shipped | fla |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 512 | **2232.3** | 2114.0 (0.945x) |
| 2048 | **2213.6** | 2091.8 (0.943x) |
| 8192 | **2148.3** | 2048.8 (0.955x) |

**9.3x on the kernel, 0.93-0.95x on the model.** Three guesses at why, two of
them mine and both wrong, then the measurement:

1. *"CPU cannot feed 6913 launches"* — no. The ratio is **flat in sequence
   length** (0.934 / 0.927 / 0.933 at 512 / 2048 / 8192); a launch-starved GPU
   would catch up as each launch did more work.
2. *"fla's Python wrapper is expensive"* — no, and backwards: per layer at
   T=512 its host time is **0.395 ms against our torch chunk path's 2.867**.
   (That comparison also caught me measuring the wrong thing — the shipped path
   is the fused TileLang kernel, whose host cost is near zero, not the torch
   one.)
3. **The gaps between kernels**, which `GPU-busy` does not contain:

| | wall | GPU-busy | **gaps** | kernels | gap/kernel |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| shipped | 239.7 ms | 235.4 | **4.3** | 3836 | **1.12 us** |
| fla | 255.8 ms | **193.5** | **62.3** | 6908 | **9.02 us** |

fla saves 42 ms on the GPU and spends 58 ms not being on it. And the count is
only 1.8x — **the per-gap cost is 8x**, because every Triton launch carries its
autotune lookup, grid computation and argument packing.

So the algebra wins and the delivery mechanism gives it all back. A TileLang
port removes exactly that: our own kernels sit at 1.12 us between launches.
Carrying fla's 193.5 ms of GPU work at our gap rate is
193.5 + 6908*1.12us = **201 ms against 239.7 — 1.19x, prefill 2232 -> ~2660
tok/s.** That is the port's payoff, measured rather than estimated.

So the honest read of "look at what sglang does" is not "adopt fla" — it is
that fla proves the 9.3x exists in the algebra, and that capturing it requires
the fusion, not the library. Which is also what AGENTS.md requires: fla is
Triton and CUDA-only, and the contract is one TileLang source for
cpu/cuda/rocm/metal, with SOTA kernels **copied, not imported**.

The measurement path stays (env-gated, off by default) because it is the only
way to re-check that number after the port.

## Two earlier rejections of this exact idea, and what separates them

The tree already holds
[2026-08-25-gdn-prefill-wy-rejected](../errors/2026-08-25-gdn-prefill-wy-rejected.md)
(2.6x slower) and
[2026-08-25-gdn-chunked-gdr-rejected](../errors/2026-08-25-gdn-chunked-gdr-rejected.md)
(0.90x, and 26% wrong at model scale). Both are TileLang ports of chunkwise-WY,
both rejected, and I nearly started a third without reading them — the pointer
is in `make_gdn_chunk_fused`'s own docstring.

They are not refuted by today's measurement. What they locate is different from
what I assumed. The first one's breakdown:

| | GPU ms, T=512 |
|---|---:|
| serial chunk (shipped) | 4.73 |
| **WY kernel A** (chunk-parallel solve) | **1.62** |
| **WY kernel B** (inter-chunk state scan) | **10.76** |

**The chunk interior was already 2.9x FASTER than the serial kernel. The
inter-chunk state scan is what lost, by 2.3x on its own.** And that is exactly
where fla differs: its `chunk_..._fwd_kernel_h_blockdim64` costs **1.70 ms
across all 48 layers** where that port spent 10.76 ms on ONE layer's worth of
work.

Measured in isolation rather than inferred from two profiles
(`scripts/probe_state_scan.py`): fla's `chunk_gated_delta_rule_fwd_h` at our
shapes (T=512, 8 chunks, 48 heads, DK=DV=128) is **37.8 us for one layer**,
1.81 ms across 48, against that port's **10760 us for one layer** — **285x**.

The reference shows why, and it is one design decision: in
`tilelang/examples/gdn/example_chunk_delta_h.py` the state lives in a
**fragment across the whole chunk loop** and never returns to memory —
`block_DV=32` exists to slice DK x 128 into register-sized DK x 32 pieces — so a
chunk costs two gemms (`V_new = U - W S`, then `S += K^T V_new`) and zero state
round-trips. A port that writes the 64 KB state back per chunk pays 285x for it.

So the earlier verdicts stand for the implementations they measured, and the
dividing line is the inter-chunk scan, not the WY algebra. The second entry's
Rule — "don't replace a serial scan when the state fits in L2 and heads >=
SMs/2, the O(T*C) extra FMAs cost more" — is the part today's 42 ms of GPU-busy
savings contradicts, and it should be re-derived against fla's `chunk_delta_h`
rather than assumed either way.

Its precision finding is NOT contradicted and is the live risk: that pipeline
staged bf16 intermediates between six kernels and read **26% error at scale=1.0**
(0.8% at 0.1). fla measures 7.3e-03 here — but our probe used scale-1 inputs
through `gdn_chunk_core`, so that specific trap is already covered.

## Rule

Read the reference implementation before estimating the gap to it. Four
successive estimates of this lever — 6.4x, 2.7x, a 2x interval — cost more
than the twenty minutes it took to find that fla is already installed, matches
our shapes, and answers the one question I had called unanswerable.
