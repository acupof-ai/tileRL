# M-row GEMV: batching 2 requests was slower than batching none — 2026-08-29

> Status: Shipped, default `_MGEMV = 3` (`TILERL_MGEMV` A/Bs it, 0 = off).

## Context

`Backend._plan` buckets a linear by M: M=1 to a GEMV, 2 <= M <= 16 to the
`mma8` decode GEMM. `mma8` is Marlin-style and **pads M to 8 rows
unconditionally**, so it costs the same at M=2 as at M=8. Measured decode
replay, 27B, 64 layers, H20 GPU 7, `scripts/profile_verify_replay.py`:

| B (W=1) | replay | aggregate tok/s |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 11.19 ms | 89 |
| 2 | 27.23 ms | **73** |
| 4 | 27.58 ms | 145 |
| 8 | 27.13 ms | 295 |

Two concurrent requests were slower **in aggregate** than one. The bench swept
`--batches 1,8`, so both measured rows looked healthy and the whole loss region
between them was invisible.

## What Worked

Give the GEMV a compile-time row count. The C tile helper
(`tl_fp4_gemv_tiles_m<G,M>` / `tl_fp8_gemv_tiles_m<G,M>`) issues the weight
loads first, decodes each tile ONCE, and then loops M rows of X against the
decoded registers — so the weight bytes, which is what the kernel streams, do
not scale with M.

| M | fp4 gemv | fp4 mma8 | fp8 gemv | fp8 mma8 | replay gemv | replay mma8 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 19.4 us | — | 18.1 us | — | **11.19 ms** | — |
| 2 | 32.2 | 51.0 | 29.0 | 37.4 | **17.54** | 27.23 |
| 3 | 49.0 | 51.1 | 40.7 | 37.5 | **27.06** | 29.68 |
| 4 | 60.8 | 51.1 | 49.9 | 37.6 | 30.14 | **27.58** |
| 8 | 117.1 | 51.2 | — | 38.1 | 55.77 | **27.13** |

- **B=2: 27.23 -> 17.54 ms, 1.55x.**
- **M=3: 29.68 -> 27.06 ms, 1.10x.**
- M=4 loses, so the default cut is 3.
- M=1 is unchanged (11.19 vs 11.20 before) — the warp-shuffle reduce that
  replaced `tvm_thread_allreduce` is neutral on the shipped path.

The GEMV's cost model is **6.6 + 13.8*M us** (fp4): only a third of it is the
weight stream, two thirds is per-row FMA. That is why it cannot simply absorb
more rows, and why the crossover sits at 3 rather than at 8.

End to end, paired in ONE process (`bench_batch_decode`, 64 layers, graph
decode, `TILERL_MGEMV=0` vs `3`), aggregate tok/s:

| B | off | on | |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 86.6 | 86.8 | 1.00x |
| 2 | **71.9** | **111.4** | **1.55x** |
| 3 | 108.2 | 119.7 | 1.11x |
| 4 | 141.9 | 142.1 | 1.00x |
| 8 | 204.3 | 210.8 | 1.03x |

## Two TileLang constraints found on the way

- `tvm_thread_allreduce`'s output must be a **zero-indexed** buffer
  (`Check failed: is_zero(index)`), so M rows would need M separate buffers.
- The eager builder rejects a Python `for` over anything but
  `range`/`T.serial`/`T.unroll`/... — so those M buffers cannot be iterated
  either (`Invalid for loop, got (0,)(<class 'tuple'>)`).

Both vanish with a `__shfl_down_sync` reduce in the C helper: the GEMV's
`reduce_thread` dim is exactly 32, so the reduction is one warp.

## Rule

Sweep the interior of a parameter, not its endpoints. `--batches 1,8` is now
`1,2,4,8` in the harness. A kernel that pads a dimension has a cost that is
flat in that dimension, and flatness is exactly what two endpoint samples
cannot see.
